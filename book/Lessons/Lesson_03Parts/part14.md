Hammett plots are very informative and are easy to analyze. Even Microsoft Excel can fit a straight line. But there are cases when we do not have a straight line. When this happens I would argue that the Hammett plot is even more informative

## During

Today we will discuss the observation of an "isokinetic temperture" in Hammett plot experiments. The idea requires accepting that in closely related systems we hace enthalpy-entropy "compensation" as we change the energy of the transition state via substitution on the benzene ring.

We will then explore curved Hammett plots. Sometimes plots can be observed to curve upward or downward. Each reveals a different aspect of the reaction kinetics

## Learning Goals
After participating in the before, during and after of this class meeting you will have explored the following learning goals of lesson \#3&hellip;

- Interpret **changes** in transition state **structure**, rate-determining step or changes in mechanism by using Hammett plots where a curved plot is observed 
- Evaluate the **suitability** of a given LFER approach for a given investigation.
- **Predict** the likely **effects** if a substituent on a **reaction** centre (inductive, resonance, field and steric effects).
- Use **Hammett plots** and other **LFER plots** to make conclusions about the structure of a **transition state** or high energy intermediate in a reaction.
- Correctly use the **Brown-Okamoto** parameters ($\sigma^+$, $\sigma^+$) when required and be able to create, line fit and interpret a **Yukawa-Tsuno** plot.
- Be able to **plot** data, obtain optimized **fit** data for your model and **use** the results.
