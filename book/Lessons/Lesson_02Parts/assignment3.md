## The Activity

This section describes the **activity** that you are to perform for the assignment. Do the following&hellip;

**Skim through** the paper given **below** and get a sense of the **reaction** being investigated and the **data set** presented by the authors as **Table 3** therein. 

"The Catalyzed Dehydration of Acetaldehyde Hydrate, and the Effect of Structure on the Velocity of Protolytic Reactions." R.P. Bell, W.C.E. Higginson." *Proc. Royal Soc. Lond. Ser. A*, **1949**, *197*, 141-159. [https://www.jstor.org/stable/98240](https://www.jstor.org/stable/98240)

**Use the data** from Table 3 in the paper to create a **Brønsted plot** by plotting log $k$ vs $pK_a$.
You won't need to type out the data from table 3, it is **available** for download [**here**](../Lesson_02/Data2All.csv) as a *csv* file. 

The **Brønsted plot** data will relate the rate constants when using a series of different acids (or bases) to catalyze a reaction to the $pK_a$ values of those acids. Use the **Brønsted plot** and obtain a value for $\alpha$ (or $\beta$). Use this information to discuss the reaction **mechanism** and explain how the data **supports** the hypothesis.

**Answer** the following questions in the **narrative** of your report. **Do not use point form** or a numbered list. Just make sure that your **short discussion** addresses these points.

1. Write out the reaction being investigated and **propose a mechanism**.
1. Why does your **Brønsted plot** differ from the one presented in Figure 4 in the paper?
2. What **choices** did the authors make that resulted in Figure 4? **Duplicate** Figure 4 using the **methodology** of the authors and briefly explain how the authors used the data.
3. If you had too much time on your hands, how would you treat the data in **replotting** for a **fresh take** on this data set from the start of the atomic age?
4. What $\alpha$ value did you obtain from your **Brønsted plot**?
5. Does the data **support** your mechansim? Is the reaction **specific or general** acid-catalyzed? How does the $\alpha$ value **support** your claim?
4. How did the authors **measure the progress** of the reaction?

You will have **replotted** the data, **investigated** why your initial plot did not duplicate the authors' plot, and ultimately **duplicated** the authors' plot using the reported methods. This is **good practice** for the final project in this course where you will **explore a literature topic** and reproduce the data analysis.
