# Overview of Lessons

This course is organized into seven major sections called "lessons". Each lesson features a theme topic and may consist of two to six class meetings. Each lesson includes one or two assignments related to the topic and class discussion.

The lessons are described below.

## [Lesson 1: Remembering the Past](./Lesson-01.md)

Our first five class meetings will focus on reviewing organic chemistry and setting the stage for physical organic chemistry. Topics include: reviewing molecular structure & conformational analysis, molecular orbital theory, transition state theory, and a review of mechanisms and "arrow pushing." 

## [Lesson 2: Acid/Base Equilibrium \& Reaction Kinetics](./Lesson-02.md)

Acids and bases are core concepts in chemistry. We have seen many examples of reaction mechanisms that require strongly acidic or basic conditions. Reaction Kinetics are also an important tool for investigating mechanism. Catalysis is a major topic in chemistry and we will introduce that idea using examples of acid and base catalysis. 

This lesson has five class meetings. Topics include: a review of Brønsted acid/base theory and an introduction to acidity functions, an introduction to how structure changes the acidity of functional groups (structure-activity relations are the core concept throughiut this course), a review of reaction kinetics with examples of complex rate laws, general and specific catalysis mechanisms with acid and base catalysis, and examples of pH-rate profiles.

## [Lesson 3: Linear Free Energy Relationships](./Lesson-03.md)

Correlating structure with activity is the major central unifying idea in this course. We can measure how structural changes affect a chemical property and use that measurement as a score for those changes. Then we can correlate this score with how the same changes affect another system. Quite often we have a linear relationship showing whether the change has a lesser or greater effect in then other system. We can use these linear relationships to probe transition state structure and investigate reaction mechanisms.

Over six class meetings we will explore the following topics: an introduction to the Hammett equation and how it can be used to correlate a set of literature values determined from pK{sub}'a' values with rates or equilibrium contants in otherb systems, scoring systems for steric effects, solvent effects and other changes, how to interpret these LFER results to invesitgate mechanisms, and a quick overview of other methods and strategies for investigating reaction mechanisms. 

## [Lesson 4: Isotope Effects](./Lesson-04.md)

This lessons occurs over two class meetings and presents the idea of isotopic substitutions to investigate reaction mechanism. 

## [Lesson 5: Substitutions & Rearrangements](./Lesson-05.md)

lessons 1 to 4 present the heart of this course. After those lessons, we will be applying what we have learned as we explore examples of reaction, both familiar and new, to did deep into the mechanisms. 

Over five class meetings we will explore examples of reactions. Every year is different and your suggestions for topics are welcome. This year I plan to explore details of solvolysis (S{sub}'N'1, S{sub}'N'2, and in-between - did you know about in-between?), and non-classical cations (a famous controversy in the field of physical organic chemistry).

## [Lesson 6: Addition & Eliminations](./Lesson-06.md)

Again, I welcome suggestions. Over these five class meetings, my current plan is to explore a series of examples and experimental evidence for mechanism in acyl substitutions (addition, acid and base catalysis), and hemiacetal chemistry (eliminations, acid catalysis). We will also explore an example of a hetercyclic ring rearrangement that demonstrates both acid and base catalysis. Examples of enzyme catalysis will also be introduced.

## [Lesson 7: Pericyclic Reactions](./Lesson-07.md)

The final five class meetings will explore examples of mechanisms that are defined by molecular orbital theory. Topics include cycloadditions, electrocyclic rearrangements, sigmatropic rearrangements, and carbene chemistry.
