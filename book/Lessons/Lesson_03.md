# Lesson 3: Linear Free-Energy Relationships

In the **previous lesson** we used the $pK_a$ value of a series of acids to create a **Brønsted plot** for an acid-catalyzed reaction. We **correlated** the free-energy difference for **dissociation** ($\log{K_a}$) of the acid with the free-energy of **activation** for the reation ($\log{k_{obs}}$). This log-log plot was **linear**. There was a direct correlation between the two free energy differences. The equilibrium (acid dissociation) correlates **directly** to the rate of the acid-catalyzed reaction. The Brønsted plot was your first experience in **linear free-energy relationships** (LFER).

**In this lesson** we will explore various LFER methods that will enable us to **infer information** about reaction **mechanism** and transition-state **structure**. 

**Louis Hammett** is often called the father of physical organic chemistry. In the **first lesson** of this course we were introduced to the **Hammett** acidity function, $H_0$. Now we will begin this lesson with an examination of the most common LFER approach, the **Hammett plot**. We will then examine other ways LFERs have been used. 

Finally, we briefly explore a grab-bag of **experimental methods** that have been used in the literature to elucidate mechanism.

### Class Meetings

12. **Substituent Effects**
    - The **theory of LFER** will be introduced. The **Hammett plot** relates the $K_a$ values of **substituted** benzoic acids to the equilibrium or rate constants of a reaction that involves a similar **substituted** benzene ring. This can be generalized to create a scoring system relates the effect of the **substituent** in benzoic acid to the effect of the same **substituent** in many other systems. This generalized **substituent** parameter is called the **Hammett substiuent parameter**, $\sigma$.
13. **Resonance Effects**
    - The Hammett substituent parameter, $\sigma$, was established in systems that did not include significant electron distribution by resonance. Direct **electron sharing by resonance** can have a **large effect** on the free-energy of a system. A series of substituent parameters that incorporate this effect, when present, have been established by **Brown and Okamoto** and others. We will introduce the $\sigma^+$ and $\sigma^-$ substituent parameters and the two-parameter system developed by **Yukawa and Tsuno** that allows for these parameters to be mixed with Hammett $\sigma$ values will be presented.
14. **Interpretting Hammett Plots**
    - We will use these $\sigma$, $\sigma^+$ and $\sigma^-$ substituent parameters to investigate **more examples** of reaction mechanism. We will also explore how **linear** free-energy plots can be **curved** and how to **interpret** these interesting results when they are observed.
15. **Steric Effects**
    - Depending on the reaction, a substituent may affect the free-energy electronically through **inductive** ($\sigma$) and **resonance** ($\sigma^+$ \& $\sigma^-$) effects. Hammett cleverly designed his systems to avoid the possibility of significant steric interaction. However, **sterics** are often a factor in many systems. How can we score the effect of **steric** interference on the free-energy of a reaction? Toady we will introduce the work of **Taft and Ingold** and the $E_s$ **steric substituent parameter**.
16. **Solvent Effects**
    - There are many more things that can change the free-energy of a reaction. Differing the **solvent** can affect reactions where charge of polarity is changing. How can we score the effect of a **solvent** on a reaction. Today we will explore the work of **Grunwald and Winstein** and the **solvent** parameter, $Y$. We will also present to work of **Swain and Scott**, who explored the idea of a score for **nucleophilicity** of a solvent, and by extension, **nucleophiles** in general with the **nucleophilicity** parameter, $n$.
17. **Investigating Reaction Mechanisms**
    - We will **summarize** the **LFER** systems that we have explored and use them in some **examples** of **investigating** reaction mechanisms.

## Learning Goals

After completing this lesson by **reading** the suggested textbook sections, **practicing** the problems, **participating** in class exercises and performing the **activities** of the assignments, you should be able to&hellip;

- **Describe** what the parameters of various LFER plots measure and how the **reference** reactions were used to establish these parameters.
    - Understand how the **Hammett** substituent parameters, $\sigma_m$ \& $\sigma_p$ were determined.
    - Understand how the **Brown-Okamoto** substituent parameters, $\sigma^+$ \& $\sigma^-$ were determined and how the **Yukawa-Tsuno** "r" value is used to interpret cases of partial resonance effect.
    - Understand how acid and base-catalyzed ester hydrolysis was used to determine the **Taft steric** substituent parameter, $E_s$, and the corresponding parameter for the **electronic substituent effect**, $\sigma^*$.
    - Understand how the **Grunwald and Winstein** solvent parameter, $Y$. is determined.
    - Understand the **Swain and Scott** parameter for **nucleophilicity**.
- Evaluate the **suitability** of a given LFER approach for a given investigation.
- **Predict** the likely **effects** if a substituent on a **reaction** centre (inductive, resonance, field and steric effects).
- Use **Hammett plots** and other **LFER plots** to make conclusions about the structure of a **transition state** or high energy intermediate in a reaction.
- Correctly use the **Brown-Okamoto** parameters ($\sigma^+$, $\sigma^+$) when required and be able to create, line fit and interpret a **Yukawa-Tsuno** plot.
- Be able to **plot** data, obtain optimized **fit** data for your model and **use** the results.
- Be able to **propose an experiment** for confirming a proposed mechanism.
